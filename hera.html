01

The starting point

It started with paper.

Operational records and processes lived across physical files and documents. As information accumulated, retrieval, tracking and maintaining a coherent operational view became increasingly difficult.

  1. Physical files + documents
  2. Growing records
  3. Difficult retrieval
  4. Need for structure

02

Discovery before development

Before designing screens, we had to understand the operation.

  1. Stakeholder visit
  2. Listen
  3. Map processes
  4. Filter requests
  5. Define requirements

We met stakeholders in person, listened to how daily operations worked, combined their requests with our own analysis, and translated the underlying needs into structured requirements.

What are they asking for — and what do they actually need?

04

From request to product requirement

“A user request and a product requirement are not always the same thing.”

Health records introduced a more complex requirement: answers could not be represented by a single data type. Depending on the metric, the system needed to support text, yes/no states, numeric values and explanatory information.

Request → requirement Real-world health information ↓ Different metric types ↓ Flexible data structure
TextYes / NoNumericExplanation

05

From requirements to a working product

The result was not a prototype left on a presentation slide.

The application brought student records, health information, attendance, activities and other operational processes into one management system.

06

Delivery was part of the product work

The product did not end when development ended.

  1. Test
  2. Install
  3. Present
  4. Train
  5. Support
  6. Collect feedback

After testing, the application was installed at Tazelenme University. I presented the product, trained users, supported early usage and collected feedback. Requests were evaluated rather than implemented automatically: suitable improvements became V1 revisions or future backlog items.

Feedback triage
Feedback Small V1 revisions V2 backlog

07 · Outcome

  • 8operational modules
  • Real-world deployment
  • User training & support
  • Feedback → product evolution

HERA moved beyond a classroom concept and into real operations, creating the foundation that later informed HERA V2.

What I learned

Good product work is not about implementing every request.

It is about understanding the problem behind the request, translating it into requirements, and making decisions that work for both users and the product.
