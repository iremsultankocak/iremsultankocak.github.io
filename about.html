I'm a Computer Engineering graduate building my career at the intersection of users, business needs, and technology. What interests me most is understanding a problem, making sense of competing requirements, and helping a team turn that understanding into a useful product.

My interest in product work grew through hands-on projects. With HERA, I worked on translating university operations into product requirements, supporting the rollout of a desktop application, and using feedback to shape the scope of its web-based successor. With AdSphere, I helped structure an influencer marketing workflow around roles, negotiations, approvals, and business rules. These experiences showed me how much product quality depends on the decisions made before and during implementation.

My technical education helps me communicate with developers and understand implementation constraints. I also enjoy the human side of the work: asking questions, aligning expectations, documenting decisions, and finding clarity when a process feels complicated.

How I approach product problems

I start by understanding the people and workflows involved. Then I look for the decisions that matter: what needs to happen, who is responsible, where the edge cases are, and how we can tell whether the solution is useful. I value clear documentation, practical collaboration, and feedback over assumptions.

  1. What needs to happen
  2. Who is responsible
  3. Where the edge cases are
  4. How we can tell whether the solution is useful
