01

The reason to evolve

V2 did not start from a blank page.

HERA V1 had already been installed and used. That experience surfaced new requirements, while interest from other Tazelenme Universities highlighted the limits of a standalone, local desktop setup. The next step was to rethink the product for wider access and more complex institutional workflows.

Input 01

Operational feedback

Real users identified friction, missing capabilities and workflow refinements.

Input 02

Broader applicability

Interest beyond the original institution prompted web-oriented product thinking.

Input 03

Research needs

Survey reuse and research data required a clearer structure than V1 provided.

02

My approach

Turn feedback into product decisions, not just a longer feature list.

  1. Observe V1 usage
  2. Collect & filter needs
  3. Define workflows
  4. Set rules & access
  5. Document V2 scope

I contributed to feature scope, module boundaries, business rules, roles and permissions, and requirements documentation. These decisions were made collaboratively; my focus was on making operational needs explicit enough to guide product development.

03

Four product decisions · a selection

Where the product became more intentional.

Four examples from the 60+ product decisions defined across the project — chosen to show different kinds of reasoning.

01Reusability

A survey pool separate from research

Problem
Rebuilding questions for each research project would duplicate work and blur the difference between a survey and the study using it.
Decision
Define a reusable survey pool, separate from research management, so students can complete assigned surveys without accessing research administration.
Survey library → reusable survey → multiple studies

02Permissions

Student self-service, with boundaries

Problem
Keeping all updates with administrators creates unnecessary work, but unrestricted editing introduces risk.
Decision
Let students view relevant courses and activities and update selected information, while preserving role-based restrictions.
Student access ≠ full admin access

  • Relevant courses & activities
  • Selected personal & health fields
Outside the boundary
  • Full admin access
  • Research administration

03Module boundaries

Clubs are not the same as events

Problem
Treating every activity alike would obscure different participation patterns and administrative needs.
Decision
Separate recurring club workflows from event-specific management.
Two participation patterns
ClubOngoing participation & attendance
EventOne-time activities & external guests

04Domain logic

Progression beyond a conventional GPA

Problem
A standard academic grading model does not fully describe a social-responsibility learning program for older adults.
Decision
Define academic progression and transcript logic around the program's actual rules, not just grades.

The work established a structured set of requirements, workflows and business rules for HERA's web evolution. The case study focuses on analysis and product definition; it does not claim a production rollout or measured adoption of V2.

What I learned

Product evolution is not adding more features. It is redefining how the system should work.

V1 taught us what real operations required. V2 challenged us to turn that learning into clearer boundaries, permissions and reusable structures.
